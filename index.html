02 — About

A bit more about how I work

Data Analyst · Data Scientist · BI/AI

I build the systems that turn messy data into decisions people actually trust.

I've worked across e-commerce, healthcare, energy, and now a reforestation nonprofit — cleaning up datasets that started out messy, building pipelines that hold up on real data instead of clean samples, and shipping models teams actually use. What stays consistent across every project is starting from what the business needs, then building whatever gets there: a SQL pipeline, a dashboard, or a full ML model.

Shipping something people actually use matters more to me than chasing a perfect model that sits in a notebook.

Business Impact

At TCS, tracing chatbot and onboarding data back to real product changes — the kind that moved user satisfaction and mobile adoption — showed me what actually matters: real impact, not just an accuracy number. That's the bar I hold every project to now.

Problem-Solving

When a model underperforms, I go back and look at the data again before I touch a single hyperparameter — almost every time, that's where the real problem turns out to be.

Technical Depth

SQL and Python are what I use every day. Past that, it depends on the project — XGBoost or LSTM when I'm forecasting something, KMeans or Isolation Forest for segmentation and anomaly work, and Power BI, Tableau, or a React dashboard depending on who's actually going to read the output.

Clear Communication

I translate models into something a stakeholder can act on immediately, no statistics background required. I've explained enough of them to non-technical teams to know the summary matters more than the math behind it.

InsightPulse — AI Customer Intelligence Platform

Problem: Small e-commerce sellers have raw transaction data but no easy way to turn it into decisions — most analytics tools assume clean, pre-formatted input they don't have.

Forecasting

Electricity Demand Forecasting — NY State

Problem: NY utility planners need reliable multi-day demand forecasts, but electricity demand swings heavily with weather in ways simple time-series models don't capture.

Approach: Joined NYISO load data with NOAA weather data, engineered 15+ temporal and weather features, and benchmarked four models before landing on XGBoost at 2.06% MAPE.

Unique approach: Benchmarked four modeling techniques (SARIMA, Prophet, LSTM, XGBoost) head-to-head instead of committing to one upfront, letting the data decide which approach actually fit the demand patterns.

Hospital Readmission Analytics

Problem: Preventable 30-day readmissions hurt patient outcomes and hospital costs, but flagging at-risk patients before discharge takes more than intuition.

Approach: Analyzed 100K+ patient records, built a logistic regression model evaluated on ROC/AUC, and deployed it through an R Shiny dashboard clinicians could use directly.

Unique approach: Chose logistic regression deliberately over a black-box model so clinicians could see exactly which factors drove each patient's risk score, not just a number.
